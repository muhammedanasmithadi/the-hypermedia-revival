Section 02

How the idea got lost

The loop you stepped through did not appear in 2020. Someone invented it in 1965, someone named it in 2000, and half of an industry forgot it by 2011. This section walks that path, and it ends asking what was actually lost.

Ted Nelson coined two words in 1965: hypertext, and the broader idea, hypermedia. Branching text. Media that join. Documents that point at other documents, forever.

In 1989 Tim Berners-Lee proposed that thought in a CERN document. Linked documents, read by a client nobody owned. Later, the hypertext carried both facts and controls, and that mix was the part that would get lost.

In 2000 Roy Fielding named that style in his doctoral dissertation: REST. Section 01 quoted his words. Here is the part most copies missed: the controls must travel with the facts, or the architecture is not REST.

Fix the dates in a line, then read the beats from top to bottom.

  1. 1965 Nelson names itTwo words: hypertext, and media that join.
  2. 1989 CERN proposes itLinked documents, read by a client nobody owns.
  3. 2000 REST gets a nameFielding ties the pattern to the web in his dissertation.
  4. 2005 The AJAX turnPages update in place; the word API replaces the word server.
  5. 2011 Contract-firstSwagger fixes the shape; HATEOAS shrinks to a links array.

Then came the detour. Enterprise software in the 2000s wanted reliability, so it standardized the conversation into rigid contracts. A schema described every message; a stack generated the code. The server answered with data, never with the next move.

In 2005 the shift happened inside a page, invisibly. AJAX let a site update in place without a reload, and the word “API” replaced “server” in ordinary talk. Nobody voted to stop sending controls. Each request quietly chose data instead.

Name the reasons plainly before judging. Native apps needed a server that spoke their language. Screens wanted movement that full reloads could not give. Separate teams wanted contracts they could test. Each reason made sense on its own.

The loss happened in how the word got used. Fielding’s acronym, HATEOAS, shrank to a JSON field of links. A link you must already know is a note, not a control. Strawman “REST” came to mean facts alone, spelled with verbs.

Fielding watched and objected on the public record. In 2008 he wrote the piece Section 01 quoted: REST APIs must be hypertext-driven. The name survived its author. The behavior did not.

2011 sealed the bargain. Swagger standardized the shape of the conversation, field by field. Renamed OpenAPI in 2016, it still gates the conversation today. Teams received exact expectations and gave up the open-ended loop. The document that carried controls ceded to the schema that described data.

One thread never died. Documentation sites, forums, and plain web apps kept the original loop running, decade after decade. A new wave of tooling, htmx and Hotwire among it, re-learned what the terminal always knew: send the document, let it carry the moves.

Now the two stories stand side by side. The terminal never stopped working; the industry built a parallel path and forgot why it existed.

Ask what each decade actually bought. Which parts of the JSON decade were necessity, and which were fashion?

That is Section 03, where both architectures sit on the same table, weighed in the open.
